# Appendix E — The Atlas: The Semantic Algebra of Famous Texts

---

## E.1 Purpose and Reading Guide

This appendix applies the full two-channel method — S(E) = ⟨ι, P⟩, the discrimination test, and the calibrated rules of §9.8 — to forty-seven expressions the reader already knows: scripture and sapiential aphorism, poetry, science, philosophy, political rhetoric, advertising, proverbs, legal declarations, popular culture, and esoterica. The heterogeneity is the point. The central claim of this book is that invariants survive the change of domain; an atlas that strips texts from ten domains with one method does not illustrate that claim — it performs it.

Each entry is a compact card:

```
[N] text — source, date | category
ι: invariant (or ∅)  ·  𝔉_d / 𝔉_eff  ·  P: payload (or [])
class | κ | flags
commento: the structural point
```

Five entries are then re-presented in full depth (§E.5) as worked exemplars of the four quadrants. Three entries are declared classification edges and presented with both readings (§E.6). The atlas closes with a double index (§E.7): by category and — more usefully — by invariant, so that each ι of the library can be consulted through its exemplars.

A methodological warning the reader should carry through these pages: a low κ is not a moral verdict on a text or its author, and a Device classification is not an accusation. The method reads structure — what an expression carries and what it does — not intent, and several of the most effective teaching acts in this atlas are devices in the precise technical sense of §9.8.

## E.2 Selection Criteria and Taxonomy

The selection was fixed before analysis, on declared criteria: (1) notoriety — the reader should recognise most entries on sight; (2) source anchorability — documented origin, with three deliberate exceptions whose unverifiable attribution is itself analysed; (3) brevity and quotability; (4) coverage — all ten categories, all four quadrants of the ⟨ι, P⟩ matrix, all ten invariants of the library; (5) declared edge cases, including one contradictory proverb pair and three borderline classifications. Three synthetic type-specimens (a recruiting pitch, an urgency advertisement, and the 1948 Forer text) serve as controls and are marked as such.

Categories: sapienziale/scritture · poesia · scienza/matematica · filosofia · retorica politica · pubblicità/marketing · specimen di controllo · proverbi/aforismi · giuridico/dichiarazioni · cultura pop · esoterica.

## E.3 Method Note

Entries 1–15 below were stripped in the calibration experiment: three independent blind runs per text over a sealed protocol with pre-registered falsifiers (no false positive on the clean control set; full convergence 60%, convergence including partials 93%; the single non-convergent entry is the borrowed-carrier hard case, whose divergence is itself the measured phenomenon — §9.8). Entries 16–47 are single-run productions under the calibrated rules by three independent analysts on mixed category sets, with the spread, scatter, and annotation rules of §9.8 in force. Inter-run records and bifurcations are preserved in the validation dataset.

### E.5.2 — The extractive device: De Beers, 1947
